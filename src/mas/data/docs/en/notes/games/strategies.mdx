A strategy is a function from states to actions: intuitively, it guides a player through a game. Formally:

- a strategy for the <Badge text="MAX" variant="info" /> player is a function $$\sigma: \mathcal{S} \rightarrow \mathcal{A}$$ that satisfies <br /> $$\sigma(s) \in \text{Actions}(s)$$ for all states $$s \in \mathcal{S}$$ for which Player$$(s)$$ is <Badge text="MAX" variant="info" />.
- a strategy for the <Badge text="MIN" variant="tip" /> player is a function $$\sigma: \mathcal{S} \rightarrow \mathcal{A}$$ that satisfies <br /> $$\sigma(s) \in \text{Actions}(s)$$ for all states $$s \in \mathcal{S}$$ for which Player$$(s)$$ is <Badge text="MIN" variant="tip" />.

#### Winning Strategies

A _winning strategy_ is a strategy that guarantees a win for the player who uses it, irrespective of how the opponent plays. A few questions should feel fairly natural once this definition is presented:

- Does a game always have a winning strategy for some player?
- How do we find a winning strategy for a given game?
- Given a strategy, how do we know if it is winning?

In fact, the idea of a winning strategy feels really ambitious, so it would be fair to wonder if we could ever come up with one. Note that there are two slightly distinct questions here: the _existence_ of a winning strategy vs the _availability_ of one that we can actually put to use. It's nice to have concrete examples of winning strategies, so try figuring out one for Nim.

<Aside variant="caution" title="Winning Strategies in Nim (Warm Up)">
For the game of Nim, work out winning strategies for:
- when there are two heaps at play
- when there are $$k$$ heaps but each heap has one or two tokens
- the general game
</Aside>

<Aside variant="caution" title="Winning Strategies in Nim (Spoiler)">
For the game of Nim, it can be shown that there is a winning strategy for the <Badge text="MAX" variant="info" /> player &mdash; i.e, the player who starts &mdash; if and only if the XOR of the sizes of all heaps is non-zero.
</Aside>

#### Existence of Winning Strategies

For the kinds of games we are working with right now, it turns out that there is always a winning strategy for one of the players.

<Aside variant="tip" title="Existence of Winning Strategies">
**Theorem.** _(Informal Statement)_ 

In a finite, deterministic, two-person game of perfect information in which the players move alternately, if the game cannot end in a draw, then one of the two players must have a winning strategy.
</Aside>

Here's a version for games that can possibly end in a draw:

<Aside variant="tip" title="Wins or Draws">
**Theorem.** _(Informal Statement)_ 

In a finite, deterministic, two-person game of perfect information in which the players move alternately, then either one of the two players has a winning strategy or both players have a drawing strategy.
</Aside>

#### Computing Winning Strategies

Although winning strategies exist, they are not always easy to find. Indeed, it is often computationally intractable to find winning strategies for many games.
